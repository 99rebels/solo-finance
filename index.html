What it does

Everything your accountant asks for in January. Or November.

Built around how sole traders actually spend: a dozen software subscriptions, flights booked on the phone, a monitor from Amazon, a coffee with a client that never sent a receipt.

Every purchase, not just the ones you forwarded

Receipts, invoices, PDF attachments, refunds and credit notes, read from the emails you already have. Order confirmations, shipping notices and promos are left out.

Matched to your bank statement

Each card payment is paired with its receipt on amount and date, so PADDLE.NET* and SQ * stop being mysteries. Payments in dollars or sterling are matched after conversion and bank fees.

The missing receipts list

The payments with no receipt, in one place, before your accountant has to chase you for them. Attach a file, add a note or mark it personal.

Business or personal, asked once

Tell us once per supplier and every receipt from them follows. Phone and broadband can be part business, so only your share counts.

VAT, sorted on every receipt

VAT read from each receipt and totalled for the year. Software bought from outside the country is flagged for reverse charge, so it doesn't catch you out.

Ready for your accountant

Grouped under Form 11 headings in Ireland and SA103 in the UK, with equipment listed separately for capital allowances. A spreadsheet, a zip of PDFs, done.

Questions

Good questions to ask about anything that reads your email.

Do my emails really never leave my computer?

Yes. Solo Finance is a Chrome extension. It asks Google for read-only access, downloads the relevant emails straight into your browser and reads them there. What it finds is stored in Chrome on your computer. We don't run servers that receive your emails, receipts or statements.

The only things that touch the internet are Gmail itself, a daily exchange-rate lookup that carries nothing about you, and a licence check if you buy a tax year.

Do I need to give you my bank login?

No. You download a statement (CSV) from your bank's website or app and drop it in. It's read on your computer like everything else. No bank feeds, no third party with access to your account.

Which banks work?

Any bank that lets you download a CSV statement. Solo Finance recognises the formats from AIB, Bank of Ireland, PTSB, Revolut, N26 and Wise in Ireland, and Monzo, Starling, Tide, Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds and NatWest in the UK. For anything else, you tell it which column is the date and which is the amount, once.

How does it handle the Irish and UK tax years?

Ireland: the tax year is January to December, and spending is grouped under the Form 11 expense headings. Your return is due by 31 October, or mid-November if you file on ROS.

UK: the tax year runs 6 April to 5 April, and spending is grouped under the SA103 self-employment headings. The spreadsheet imports into Making Tax Digital software, and your Self Assessment is due online by 31 January.

Is this accounting software?

No, and it doesn't try to be. It does the part sole traders hate: finding every receipt and proving every payment. Your accountant, or your bookkeeping software, does the rest with what it exports.

What about receipts that never came by email?

Fuel, shops and trade counters often don't send one. Those payments still show up from your bank statement, on the missing receipts list. Attach a scan or photo from your computer, add a note for your accountant, or mark it personal.

Does it work with Outlook?

Gmail and Google Workspace for now, including more than one account if your receipts go to different addresses. Outlook is on the list.

What happens if I uninstall or change computer?

Your data lives in Chrome, so uninstalling removes it. Save a backup file from Settings first and restore it anywhere. Your receipts are still in Gmail either way, so a fresh scan rebuilds the purchases on its own.
